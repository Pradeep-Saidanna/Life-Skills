# Listening and Active Communication

## Active Listening
1. What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?
* Give full attention – Focus only on the speaker, avoid distractions.
* Avoid interrupting – Let the speaker complete their message.
* Use “door openers” – Encourage them (e.g., “tell me more”, “go on”).
* Show interest using body language – Nod, maintain eye contact.
* Stay mentally present – Don’t think about your reply while listening.
* Take notes if needed – Helps remember important points.
* Paraphrase the message – Repeat in your own words to confirm understanding.

## Reflective Listening
2. According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?
* Carefully listen to the speaker.
* Understand both message and feelings.
* Repeat or rephrase what they said.
* Confirm if your understanding is correct.
* Show empathy and respect.
* Avoid judging or interrupting.
* Ensure both people clearly understand each other.

## Reflection
3. What are the obstacles in your listening process?
* Distractions (phone, surroundings).
* Thinking about my response early.
* Interrupting others.
* Losing focus in long discussions.
* Making assumptions.
* Emotional reactions.
4. What can you do to improve your listening?
* Focus fully on the speaker.
* Avoid interruptions.
* Take notes during important talks.
* Ask questions for clarity.
* Reduce distractions.
* Practice summarizing.
* Stay calm and open-minded.

## Types of Communication
5. When do you switch to Passive communication style in your day to day life?
* When talking to seniors or teachers.
* When I want to avoid conflict.
* When I feel less confident.
* When I don’t want to hurt someone.
6. When do you switch into Aggressive communication styles in your day to day life?
* When I feel frustrated.
* During arguments.
* When my opinion is ignored.
* When I strongly believe I am right.
7. When do you switch into Passive Aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment and others) communication styles in your day to day life?
* When I’m upset but don’t say directly.
* Using sarcasm.
* Giving silent treatment.
* Complaining to others instead of the person.
* Indirect communication.
